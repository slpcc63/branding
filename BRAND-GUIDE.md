## Visual language

- Luminous cyan, electric blue and violet gradients, sapphire/indigo depth and light spreading inward from edges. Restrained amber adds warmth.
- Dark mode: midnight navy. Light mode: pearl or warm white, with the same product accents and readable contrast.
- Softened low-contrast card borders, generous spacing, clear hierarchy and legible text. Use glow selectively around focal artwork and controls; avoid glowing body text or bright outlines on every card.
- Preserve distinct product silhouettes and metaphors. Do not turn every product into a waveform or substitute a generic library symbol for an approved app icon.
- UI must remain intuitive: visible navigation, clear return paths, understandable states and accessible controls. Decorative treatment must support these needs.

Working reference colors: navy #0B1023; warm white #F7F8FC; electric blue #568BFF; violet #9474FF; amber #FFB547. These are reference tokens, not a claim of sampled production specifications. No final licensed typeface family is established here.

See the asset index for exact files. The geometric D is the selected company symbol, conditional on Doxle remaining the company name. Duke’s face is the Duke companion identity. Preserve earlier face-plus-Doxle lockups as historical artwork; do not use them as the current company logo. Keep the detailed Duke artwork as a backup. The shared symbol library is assets/symbols/catalog-500, with its manifest, README, four raster sizes and combined ZIP. Existing export notes document 500 named icons; this setup verifies the central paths, not a fresh per-icon QA pass. Earlier packs remain historical subsets. Preview sheets are visual catalogs: do not ship checkerboard previews or screenshots. Product-inspired library symbols do not replace approved app icons.

For production, verify actual dimensions, alpha, padding and small-size readability. Use full opaque square masters for platforms requiring app icons and let the platform apply its mask; do not bake rounded outside corners into those exports. Transparent UI symbols and brand marks are separate assets. Raster exports are not vector masters. Do not assume artwork derived from native platform catalogs is licensed for cross-platform distribution; use the independently drawn library and preserve provenance.

## Applying and maintaining this source

1. Read this guide and the relevant asset-index entries at the start of affected work; check the workbook for visual context.
2. Use the approved master. Verify project-specific requirements, existing implementation and platform export rules before integrating.
3. Preserve repository identities, bundle identifiers, stored data and compatibility. Branding changes do not authorize repository creation, technical renames, migrations, deployment or publication.
4. Record approved changes here and in the asset index, then update the workbook when its visuals change. Retain previous approved assets as clearly labeled backups. Record the decision source and date; no decision means no promotion.
5. If a chat cannot access these local files, retrieve a current authorized copy through an accessible source or report the access gap before dependent visual decisions. Do not claim to have checked inaccessible assets.

This local source is not a live broadcast. Existing chats must reload guidance before relevant work. Separate ChatGPT/cloud sessions and other computers need an accessible copy; no automatic cross-service synchronization is configured.
