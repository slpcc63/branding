# Doxle Project Branding Rules

Effective November 15, 2026. Applies to branding, naming, websites, apps, UI visual design and release artwork in the Doxle software family.

## Read before design work

1. Read the current [brand guide](BRAND-GUIDE.md) and the project's approved identity in the [asset index](assets/ASSET-INDEX.json).
2. Read the [UI style guide](guides/DOXLE-STYLE-GUIDE.md) and relevant [design-system components and patterns](design-system/DOXLE-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md).
3. Check the project's existing requirements, platform conventions and implementation. Apply the system to its actual users and tasks rather than copying a generic screen or another product's workflow.
4. State which system edition and project-specific exceptions the work uses in the ordinary design or implementation record. Preserve existing report-back and QA routes.

The workbook is the visual brand family catalog. The asset catalog identifies files and status. The style guide defines appearance. The design system combines foundations with components and behavior. Concept mockups illustrate possibilities; they do not override approved artwork or establish production acceptance.

## Naming

Use each product's exact current approved name and capitalization from the brand guide. Current names include Duke (working name), Cue, Prism, Portal, Tether, Trace, Trace for Alexa, Cadence, Sentinel, Handoff, Fuse, SIFT and Dispatch. Doxle remains a provisional company name; its selected symbol is the geometric D. Duke’s face is assigned to the Duke app, not the company logo. Store names use “[Product] | Doxle”, for example “Cue | Doxle” and “Prism | Doxle”. Home-screen labels use the bare product name, such as Cue or Prism. Shannon approved this on November 15, 2026, replacing the “by SLPCC63” qualifier.

For new naming proposals, prefer natural, meaningful single words connected to the product's purpose or a personal connection. Avoid forced combinations and generic feature descriptions. This preference does not rename existing approved exceptions. The approved store lockup places the product first and Doxle second. It does not change the underlying product name or authorize technical identifier changes.

Present candidates as proposals. Check relevant App Store/software collisions before describing a name as available; distinguish an initial search from legal clearance or successful store registration. Never infer a rename from brainstorming. Record explicit naming approval in the central guide and affected catalog entries. User-facing catalogs show current names; preserve historical records and stable asset filenames where needed for provenance and compatibility.

Marketing names do not authorize changes to repository names, project IDs, bundle IDs, schemas, stored data, signing, migration paths or integrations.

## Shared appearance

Use the established family direction: sapphire and midnight navy, luminous cyan-blue-violet edge gradients, restrained amber warmth, pearl light surfaces, softened card borders, calm text areas and clear hierarchy. Preserve the approved product symbol and its distinct metaphor. Keep glow in artwork and selected accents; do not glow body text or outline every card brightly.

Design both light and dark appearances where supported. Use semantic color roles, responsive spacing and readable typography. Follow native platform behavior and text scaling. Do not introduce a custom font or treat a mockup's sample font label as an approved typeface. The current system-font foundation and detailed numerical defaults remain proposals until approved.

Navigation must show location, a clear return path and understandable action/state feedback. Preserve relevant user context when returning. Accessibility and task clarity take precedence over decorative treatment.

## Freedom of design

Classify rules and choices explicitly. This classification framework governs interpretation; it does not promote the numerical defaults in edition 0.1 to approved designs.

| Classification | What must happen | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Required | Preserve established identity and functional usability requirements. A change to an approved identity requires explicit approval. | Exact approved artwork, readable contrast, accessible names and controls, clear navigation and return paths. |
| Default | Begin with the documented convention. Adapt for a real product/platform need and record the reason when departing. | Type scale, spacing, card padding, corner radii, density and navigation arrangement. |
| Expressive | Exercise artistic judgment within approved family character and usability limits. No separate approval is needed for every ordinary in-scope artistic choice. | Accent balance, gradient placement, hero composition, original supporting imagery and restrained motion. |

Required/Default/Expressive is separate from Proposed/Approved, and separate again from Implemented/Tested/Accepted. A proposed Required rule is still a proposal. Do not make a new style proposal mandatory by attaching a label.

Cue may be spacious and reassuring, Prism may emphasize layered data visualization, and Tether may use compact conversations. Shared foundations should make them related, not identical. For example, 24-unit card padding is a proposed default; 16 may suit a dense conversation list while readability and usable controls remain required. Ordinary adaptations do not justify redesigning the approved logo or changing product behavior.

Use existing review and record-keeping routes for exceptions. Record the affected rule, scope, reason and evidence; identify explicit approval only where an approved requirement actually changes. Do not add approval gates for routine reversible design work already authorized by Shannon.

## Artwork and files

Use approved masters. Backups, historical art, unselected alternatives, mockups and generic library symbols are not approved replacements. Cue's approved primary is the first ribbon-and-amber-point concept. Preserve intentional backups and output sizes.

Verify actual dimensions, transparency and readability at the target size. Keep app-icon exports, transparent brand marks and UI symbols distinct. Do not ship screenshots, checkerboard previews, draft badges or unreviewed placeholders as production assets. Missing originals are gaps to resolve, not permission to invent an approved replacement.
